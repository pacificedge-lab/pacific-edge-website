## Planning and operational experience

Chris's experience includes substantial planning engagements with Indigenous groups in the United States, alongside current operational systems work in Indigenous education in Canada.

That experience informs an approach that connects planning with the capacity, working arrangements and information needed to carry it forward. Each engagement starts with the organization's own priorities and context.

## Strategic priorities and initiative development

Examine the choices facing your Nation or organization and establish a practical way forward.

This can involve clarifying priorities, assessing options or developing an initiative to the point where its scope, resource requirements and next steps are understood. The resulting plan connects leadership's decisions with practical responsibilities and a sequence of work.

## Organizational capacity and operations

Build on existing strengths and identify where additional capacity would help deliver an agreed priority or support a service.

Business and service design brings the working arrangements together: roles, responsibilities, workflows, information and supporting technology. We shape the approach around your governance, available resources and specific requirements, with your team involved in defining how it will work.

## Funding pathways and project readiness

Connect defined priorities with a sequenced approach to pursuing suitable funding opportunities.

The work can include project definition, readiness requirements, funding research and pursuit priorities. The aim is to align the funding approach with the project, its delivery requirements and the organization's capacity.

## Technology and AI

Assess digital systems, information and automation in the context of the work they need to support.

An engagement can examine an existing system, identify a useful application or define a pilot. Your leadership's requirements for information access, stewardship and decision responsibilities form part of the scope.

## Continuing advice and leadership

Retain experienced support as priorities develop and implementation proceeds.

A strategic advisory arrangement provides regular access to judgment and decision support. A fractional leadership role provides defined capacity to coordinate an initiative or area of operational improvement. We keep decisions and working knowledge understandable to your team, with responsibilities reviewed as internal capability develops.

## A practical starting point

A Capacity and Implementation Roadmap can bring the work together around a department, service or defined initiative. You receive agreed requirements, a view of available capacity and gaps, responsibilities, dependencies and a sequence of next steps. This gives leadership a basis for deciding what to start, resource or develop further.

A strategic engagement may be the better starting point when the priorities or choices still need to be resolved. We agree the approach with your leadership.

## Applied work in Indigenous education

## Working in your context

We agree who sets priorities, who makes decisions and how the work fits alongside existing services and initiatives. Staff knowledge informs the design, and information use and access are agreed as part of the engagement.

The scope identifies the contribution from Pacific Edge, the role of your team and any specialist support required. Plans, tools and working knowledge should remain understandable and useful to your organization as the work develops.
