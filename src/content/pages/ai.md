Pacific Edge starts with the capability your organization needs. We assess the work and available information, then determine an appropriate approach and what reliable operation would require. Human judgment and responsibility remain explicit as automation develops.

## Where should you start?

You may have a specific application in mind, a collection of experiments, or pressure to establish an organizational approach.

We examine the work and intended benefit before recommending an approach. The assessment can lead to process changes, conventional automation or an AI application, according to what would improve the result.

## Opportunity and readiness

Identify where capability could be improved and assess whether AI would make a useful contribution.

The AI Opportunity and Readiness Assessment produces a prioritized set of opportunities, the requirements and constraints around them, and a recommendation for the first initiative. Where a pilot is appropriate, its brief defines the workflow, resources and evaluation criteria.

## Workflow and automation design

Examine a process in enough detail to understand where technology could improve it.

We define the proposed workflow, system requirements and points where people need to review information or make decisions. That creates a clear basis for a pilot or implementation assignment.

## Information and knowledge readiness

Assess whether the information an application needs is accessible, reliable and understood.

The work can address information structure, ownership, access and maintenance, alongside the requirements for finding and using organizational knowledge. Where outputs inform consequential decisions, we define how source information, generated interpretation and material uncertainty should remain visible.

## Adoption and implementation

Plan how a new application will operate in practice: who uses it, who supports it, what needs review and how its usefulness will be assessed. Set the scope of automation and the points where a person must exercise judgment, according to the consequences involved.

Support can include a defined pilot, guidance for users, implementation coordination and ongoing leadership. The scope establishes the delivery team and any specialist development or integration expertise required.

## Testing an application

A pilot should answer a decision. Does the application produce useful results in the agreed workflow, and what would further implementation require?

We agree the scope and evaluation criteria before development, test with the intended users, and use the findings to recommend the next step. A demonstration shows what is possible. Evaluation establishes how reliably it works under realistic conditions, where review is needed and whether further investment is justified.

## If AI work is already underway

An existing initiative may need help with direction, readiness, adoption or delivery. We can assess the situation and define the support needed to move it forward.
